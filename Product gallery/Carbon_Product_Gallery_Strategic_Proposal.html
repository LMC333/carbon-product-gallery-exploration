Carbon Product Gallery redefined

A quantitative and qualitative research roadmap to transform the legacy visual directory into "Carbon in Action" — a living hub bridging design blueprints and production UI.

Executive Summary: The legacy Airtable Product Gallery acted as a simple repository of static UI captures. A targeted research study surveying 28 IBM product designers and developers (alongside 3 core Carbon Design System team members) identified a clear requirement: the Product Gallery must evolve from a passive archive of images into an interactive, learning-focused visual ecosystem. This proposal translates core user insights into a strategic plan for a rebuilt gallery called "Carbon in Action", designed to accelerate Carbon V12 migration and support community-driven contributions.

Guidance Area A: Proactive discovery and marketing

Over 40% of surveyed product team members were entirely unaware of the gallery's existence or had never been prompted to use it. This feedback guides us to integrate the new gallery natively into Carbon's primary documentation navigation and promote it actively across active internal Slack workspaces.

"Be louder on Slack that IBM Carbon has their very own Mobbin clone! I've been a UX Designer here 10 years and this is the first I'm hearing of this site." — Senior UX Designer, Research Participant

Guidance Area B: Provide rich context and page-type categorization

Users are frustrated by flat screenshots that lack context. They want screenshots grouped by page archetypes (e.g., landing pages, monitoring dashboards, detail views, AI chat interfaces) rather than random uploads. They also request toggleable visual annotations and short product videos to understand layout behavior.

"I'd add the ability to show the different screens grouped by the type of pages — example: All dashboards, all reports, all AI conversational interfaces, etc." — UX Researcher, Research Participant
"When/why would I go there? It's a collection of seemingly random screenshots of different products, some of which are quite outdated. Examples lack context and design rationale... Some screenshots are difficult to view or navigate to." — Cross-Team Consensus (Common qualitative themes from over 60% of participants)

Guidance Area C: Connect users to the original builders

Product teams want to use the gallery as a peer-to-peer networking tool. If a team finds an inspiring screen, they want to contact the creators directly via Slack to ask specific questions about design choices, implementation hurdles, and customization parameters.

"Reconnecting to respective project owners as (if they currently have worked on that specific product). Guidance support and also if a designer is working on it as a beginner in respective career, they can approach them regarding that specific query or as to what product-specific challenges did they go through." — Product Designer, Research Participant

Guidance Area D: Lower contribution barriers with automation

The primary barrier to contribution is the high manual effort required to tag components, name products, and add metadata. Product teams recommend eliminating complex manual forms by leveraging Figma plugin integrations and providing clear public recognition/rewards for contributors.

"I would have to be convinced of the value it brings before investing time to contribute. I am not convinced yet. Therefore I have no plans to contribute." — Senior UI Developer, Research Participant

Stage 3: The "Carbon in Action" hub proposal

We explicitly challenge the assumption that we should simply recreate a static screenshot library. Instead, we propose "Carbon in Action", a modernized, social, and visual hub integrated natively into the Carbon ecosystem.

2. Core functional requirements

A. Bidirectional component cross-linking

MVP Production screenshots will connect directly to official Carbon component documentation. MVP Implementation: To accelerate time-to-market, components will be cataloged and filtered inside a clean sidebar panel. Target Release: An interactive overlay engine will render hotspot annotations directly over screenshots. Clicking a hotspot navigates to the component guide; conversely, documentation pages will pull live screenshots showing components in real-world context.

B. The V12 migration slider (Strategic accelerator)

MVP An interactive, sliding split-screen showing a legacy (V11) interface alongside its migrated (V12) equivalent. This provides visual reassurance to product leads that V12 upgrades maintain density and professional polish, and serves as a powerful way to demonstrate how complex user interfaces can be simplified, decluttered, and visually streamlined as part of the v12 transition.

C. Zero-friction contribution engine (AI-Powered Auto-Tagging)

MVP To eliminate manual cataloging effort without the overhead of a dedicated Figma plugin, contributors simply upload their screenshots. A lightweight AI vision model (via watsonx.ai) automatically analyzes the image, identifies standard Carbon components (e.g., Tabs, Data Table, Dropdown), drafts descriptive alt-text, and pre-populates metadata. The contributor reviews the generated tags and clicks publish, shrinking submission time to under 10 seconds.

D. Peer-to-peer Slack connectivity

MVP Every screenshot detail page embeds a contributor card displaying the team's Slack contact details and a direct button: [ Chat with Jane Doe on Slack ]. This instantly breaks down organizational silos across IBM.

3. Strategic shifts: Challenging the status quo

  • Embrace real-world pragmatism: Instead of only allowing flawless, completely compliant screens, we should showcase highly functional custom extensions. If a team had to customize a component to support an edge-case, we want that documented. It serves as invaluable research for Carbon users and for the promotion of Carbon labs.
  • Developer-centric insights: Developers make up a huge portion of Carbon's audience. We should highlight the frontend framework (React, Web Components, Svelte) and link directly to internal GitHub repository declaration files to allow instant code inspection.
  • Distributed moderation: To prevent the core Carbon team from becoming a curation bottleneck, we propose appointing "Carbon Ambassadors" across major product groups with direct self-publishing and approval rights.
